A paragraph is a self-contained unit of written prose that consists of one or more sentences focused on a single controlling idea or topic. Core Structure of a Paragraph • Topic Sentence: States the main idea or controlling point, usually placed at the very beginning. • Supporting Sentences: Expands, explains, or proves the main idea with details, evidence, or examples. • Concluding Sentence: Summarizes the main point or transitions smoothly to the next idea or paragraph. Common Types of Paragraphs • Narrative: Tells a story or recounts a sequence of events. • Descriptive: Paints a picture of a person, place, or object using sensory details. • Expository: Explains, informs, or gives instructions on a topic. • Argumentative / Persuasive: Takes a clear stance or position and provides evidence to prove it. Formatting and History • Visual Breaks: Paragraphs are typically separated by an empty line or indicated by an indentation on the first line. • The Pilcrow (¶): Historically, scribes used a marginal symbol called the paragraphos or a stylized "C" that evolved into the pilcrow (¶) mark to designate paragraph breaks. • You can learn more about writing and structuring text via resources like the UNC Writing Center or check definitions on Merriam-Webster.
